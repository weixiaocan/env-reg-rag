# Product specification template

Use only sections that improve decisions for the current project. Keep the document proportional to its scope.

## Document control

- Status: Draft | In review | Approved | Superseded
- Revision date:
- Related evidence or source documents:

## 1. Problem and context

- Current situation
- User or organizational problem
- Why it matters now
- Evidence and known limitations of that evidence

## 2. Goals and success measures

- Desired outcomes
- Measurable success indicators or agreed qualitative signals
- Baseline, when known

## 3. Users and stakeholders

- Primary users
- Secondary users
- Decision maker and acceptance owner
- Affected stakeholders

## 4. Primary workflows

Describe representative end-to-end workflows, including important failure or exception paths.

Add a few real examples only when they help validate the workflows. Label generated or hypothetical examples clearly. Building a complete golden set or benchmark belongs to evaluation design, not this document.

## 5. Requirements and acceptance criteria

Give each requirement a stable identifier and priority. Pair it with observable acceptance criteria and its source or rationale when useful.

## 6. Non-functional requirements

Include only relevant constraints such as performance, reliability, security, privacy, compliance, accessibility, operability, and cost.

## 7. Scope boundaries

- In scope
- Out of scope
- Deferred possibilities that are not commitments

## 8. Risks, assumptions, and dependencies

Separate verified facts from assumptions. State what would invalidate an assumption.

## 9. Open decisions

For each consequential unresolved item, record the question, impact, owner, and next action or decision date.

## 10. Approval

Record the approval role or decision and date without adding personal names unless explicitly required. Approval confirms the product intent and boundaries, not the technical design.
